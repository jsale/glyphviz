# Fourier Epicycles — a nested Torus chain draws its own curve

A chain of nested `TOPO_TORUS` parents **is** a Fourier series. These six scenes
show it, and — the part ANTz could never do — the pen leaves a permanent line
behind.

The construction is Jeff's, ported from an ANTz scene of a few years ago
(`fourier_v1`): five nodes, each the child of the last, every one of them Torus
topology, each spinning its `translate_x` at a constant rate. The fifth is a
plain white sphere, and it traces something far more intricate than any circle.
What GlyphViz adds is the **breadcrumb trail** — a transparent Plot-topology
parent holding one Point-geometry bead per frame at the pen's *world* position,
so the Plot polyline through those beads **is** the traced curve.

Everything here is generated by `glyphviz_core/epicycles.py`, which is also what
**Tools → Epitrochoid Generator (Ctrl+E)** drives — so these scenes and the
designer cannot drift apart, and anything you tune in the GUI can be pasted back
into the generator as a few numbers.

## The scenes

Open a `*_gv_node.csv` (File → Open), or drop the folder on the window. Keep
each folder intact: GlyphViz auto-loads Channels only when exactly one `ch-map`
and one `ch-tracks` file sit beside the node CSV.

| folder | what it shows | driven by |
|---|---|---|
| `01_rate_driven` | the ANTz original: harmonics 1, 2, 3, 4 at equal amplitude | `translate_rate_x`, trail fully drawn |
| `02_self_drawing` | the same figure, drawing itself under the pen | Channels — press **Space** |
| `03_square_wave` | a real Fourier series: odd harmonics 1, 3, 5, 7 at amplitudes 1, ⅓, ⅕, ⅐ | `translate_rate_x` |
| `04_inclined_orrery` | **Tilt** — each arm orbits in its own inclined plane | `translate_rate_x` |
| `05_torus_knot` | **Tube** — an arm riding a second circle around the torus tube | `translate_rate_x` |
| `06_deep_space` | six arms, both 3-D knobs at once, drawing itself | Channels — press **Space** |

`01`, `03`, `04` and `05` need no playback controls at all: they start moving
the moment they open and loop forever. Because their trails were computed
independently of the running animation, watching the pen ride a curve that is
already on screen *is* the correctness check, performed live.

`02` and `06` are the ones that answer "can GlyphViz do what ANTz couldn't".
Both are 360 frames — exactly one period — so the curve erases and redraws on
every loop. **They look empty until you press Space**, because at frame 0 every
bead's alpha is still 0.

For the planar scenes, the best first view is the built-in **+Z View** camera
(Shift+C cycles, or use the Camera toolbar). The 3-D ones are worth a look in
stereo. There is deliberately **no World Grid**: a grid at *z* = 0 would be
coplanar with the planar curves and z-fight with them.

## Why it is a Fourier series

Each level's `translate_x` is an angle measured in its **parent's** rotating
frame, so the angles compose down the chain. Arm *k* therefore turns at world
harmonic

```
h₁ = r₁
hₖ = hₖ₋₁ + (−1)^(k−1) · rₖ        (+ on odd levels, − on even ones)
```

Jeff's rates `(+1, −1, +1, −1)` give harmonics **1, 2, 3, 4** — a four-term
truncated Fourier series with all four amplitudes equal:

```
z(t) = 10·e^(i·1t) + 10·e^(i(2t−90°)) + 10·e^(i·3t) + 10·e^(i(4t−90°))
```

The generator asserts both the ladder and those amplitudes against the real
engine: it reloads the written CSV, runs the chain, and DFTs the pen's path.

### The load-bearing detail: `rotate = (90, 90, 0)`

Every level carries it, and the whole construction rests on it. It exactly
cancels the base rotation `TOPO_TORUS` applies to its children, which keeps
every ring in one plane and lets the angles compose. Measured with
`rotate = (0, 0, 0)` instead: the rings tip 90° out of plane at every level, the
chain corkscrews 20 units off the plane, and the ladder collapses to
`[1, 0, 0, 1]`.

The cancellation also leaves the frame's **handedness flipped** at each step —
which is why the signs alternate above, and why alternating `±1` rates *add*
rather than cancel.

`rotate_x`/`rotate_y` = 90 is one of the rotations where ANTz Heading/Tilt/Roll
and GlyphViz's own Euler XYZ agree exactly (verified both ways: same harmonics,
same in-plane rings), so the files are written in Euler XYZ — the convention
that is friendlier to hand-edit in the Properties panel afterwards.

### The starting phases are Jeff's, recovered

`fourier_v1` was saved mid-flight, with its angles already wound up to
`(8661.918, −8571.918, 8751.918, −8571.918)`. Those are `phase + rate · f` for
one unknown elapsed frame count; taking the first phase as 0 gives
`f = 8661.918` and leaves phases `(0, 90, 90, 90)` — every one an exact multiple
of 90, which no accident produces. So they were authored, and they are
reproduced here.

Phase changes the **shape** but not the spectrum. Zeroing all four gives a tamer
figure (a large circle with two lobes); `(0, 90, 90, 90)` gives the deep spiral
loop with three nested inner loops, half-span 40 units.

## The two ways out of the plane

**Tilt** (`04_inclined_orrery`) leans an arm's orbit plane out of the previous
arm's, by exactly the number of degrees you ask for — it is written as the
parent node's `rotate_y`, which is the one rotation field that tips a ring plane
at this base pose (`rotate_x` and `rotate_z` do not).

**Tube** (`05_torus_knot`) gives an arm a *second* circle riding the first,
around the torus tube. In the spectrum it is a **sideband generator**: an arm at
harmonic *h* with tube rate *r* contributes *h*, *h − r* and *h + r* in the
plane, plus pure out-of-plane motion at *r*. With two arms that is a torus knot.

The tube is carved *out* of the arm rather than added to it, so the authored arm
length stays the true arm length and the parent's **drawn ring becomes the tube
its child actually travels**. Rings thicker than a hairline draw as wireframe:
a solid torus at that size is an opaque doughnut that hides the mechanism it
exists to show.

`06_deep_space` uses both, over six arms.

## How the trail is built

Three things worth knowing before reusing the pattern:

- **The Plot parent must be transparent, not hidden.** `color_a = 0` hides its
  glyph while leaving the polyline; `hide = 1` suppresses the polyline entirely
  (`viewport._draw_topology_overlays` skips hidden plot nodes).
- **Line colour comes from the children, width from the parent.** The polyline
  takes each vertex's colour from that bead, and its width from the *parent's*
  `ratio × 20` pixels — the same convention Link lines and Point sprites use.
  Here the parent is `ratio 0.15` → a 3 px line, and the beads `ratio 0.10` →
  2 px dots that vanish into it. **The lines in the
  [topology animations](../Topology_Animation_Examples/README.md) were never
  invisible** — the beads and the line are the same colour there, so the line
  reads as one continuous stroke.
- **Row order is path order.** Plot draws through its children in CSV row order,
  which is why the beads are emitted frame by frame.

Bead positions are **sampled from the real engine** — `trace_path()` builds a
live `Scene` from the chain and reads `world_pos()` — not recomputed from a copy
of the topology math. That is why the trail lands on the curve to ~7e-07 units
(the node CSV's own 6-decimal write precision) rather than approximately, and
why tilt, tube and the ladder can never drift apart from what renders.

In the self-drawing scenes, beads share an alpha channel in groups of 3, so the
tracks file holds ~120 channels instead of 360. A group lights when its **last**
bead is reached, so the line lags the pen by at most 3 frames (a tenth of a
second at 30 fps) rather than ever running ahead of it — a trail drawing a curve
the pen has not travelled yet would be a lie.

## The one caveat: Global Scale

These scenes are authored for **Global Scale 1.0**, the app default.

`base_scale` enters placement only through a topology's base *radius* — the
Torus chain's arms are `base_scale × cumulative scale` long — while a Cartesian
parent's children (Plot included) take their translate as literal world units,
with no `base_scale` term at all. So raising Global Scale grows the pen's orbit
and leaves the trail where it was. At Global Scale 3 the orbit triples and the
trail drifts out of register.

The fix is one field: set the **trail parent's Topo Scale** to the same number.
`TOPO_PLOT` reads all three axes as distances, and `topo_scale` multiplies
exactly those.

This is not specific to these scenes — it is a property of any world-space
overlay drawn onto an angular-topology scene. The
`Topology_Animation_Examples` module carries the same assumption in its
`B = 1.0` constant.

## Designing your own

**Tools → Epitrochoid Generator (Ctrl+E)** is the same engine with a live
preview, a measured readout (ladder, spectrum, closure period, depth), eight
presets, a seeded Randomize, and Save Scene → the same CSV folders as these.
See `glyphviz-docs/docs/reference/epitrochoid-generator.md`.

To edit these scenes as code instead, the three knobs are at the top of
`_scenes()` in the generator. Note that **level *k*'s scale sets the length of
arm *k+1***, not arm *k* — a node's torus is the circle its *child* rides — but
the generator telescopes that for you, so every `length` is authored directly in
world units at any depth. A square wave is rates `(1, −2, 2, −2)` with lengths
`base/1, base/3, base/5, base/7`.

## Regenerating

```text
python generate_fourier_epicycles.py             # all six, in place
python generate_fourier_epicycles.py --out DIR   # somewhere else
python generate_fourier_epicycles.py --only 05   # one scene
python generate_fourier_epicycles.py --verify    # re-check without rebuilding
```

Every run reloads what it wrote and asserts: the trail lies on the pen's path,
the harmonic ladder matches the prediction, the curve closes, and the tag file
loaded. `tests/test_epicycles.py` and `tests/test_epicycle_gui.py` run the same
checks over every preset, plus real `ChannelEngine` playback.
