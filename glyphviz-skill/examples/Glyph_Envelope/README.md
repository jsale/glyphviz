# Glyph_Envelope — one hyperglyph at the data's minimum, median and maximum

**What it teaches:** how to stop a hyperglyph from breaking when real data
arrives. A design gets dialled in on one good-looking record, then the full
dataset goes in and parts fly apart, sink through the floor, or swallow each
other — always because the design quietly assumed a range the data does not
respect.

The discipline is four steps, and this example *is* those steps:

1. **Declare a budget per variable** — variable → channel → domain → **range**,
   clamped at both ends.
2. **Clamp on the way in**, not after something has already broken.
3. **Build the glyph at the data's minimum, median and maximum.**
4. **Measure those three and look at one**, before generating all N.

Three glyphs tell you what a thousand would, in a fraction of the time.

## The glyph

A palm tree, one per medical student, from **Dr Dave Warner's Medical
Infoscope**: a cube planter box you can grab and drag (blue = passed Step 1,
red = failed), a bare stem, then four stacked trunk segments — one per family
of tests, each thinner than the one below and coloured on a ROYGBV ramp by that
family's mean score — with a fan of bead branches off each segment carrying the
individual test scores in lift angle and bead size, and a crown sphere for the
CBSE score, sized and coloured by it. **The design is his. The data is not.**

> `data/synthetic_cohort.csv` is generated from a seeded RNG by `make_cohort()`.
> No real student records are used or bundled. The cohort exists only to give
> the envelope a domain to be measured against.

Reading left to right in the scene: **maximum** (large, violet crown, branches
lifted, blue box), **median**, **minimum** (small, orange crown, branches
drooping, red box). The direction of every encoding is the same — a struggling
record is the *smaller*, *lower* thing on screen, never the largest.

## The envelope

| variable | channel | domain | clamped range |
|---|---|---|---|
| group mean score | trunk segment colour | 0…100 | 40…95 on ROYGBV |
| group mean score | branch lift angle | 0…100 | −24°…+52° |
| individual test | branch bead radius | 0…100 | 0.21…0.46 |
| individual test | branch bead brightness | 0…100 | 0.45…1.0 |
| CBSE score | crown radius | 0…100 | 0.42…0.92 |
| CBSE score | crown colour | 0…100 | 30…90 on ROYGBV |
| Step 1 pass/fail | planter box colour | {0, 1} | blue \| red |

Everything else in the glyph is a **constant**, not a channel: segment height,
taper, bead count, bead spacing, fan radius. Keeping that line sharp is most of
the discipline — a number that does not carry data has no business varying.

Note the two colour channels are on the same ramp but the branches are *not*:
segments carry the group mean on ROYGBV, branches carry individual scores on
their **group's own hue** at varying brightness. Putting both on ROYGBV made
every group violet in the maximum glyph and the four families became
indistinguishable. Two variables cannot ride the same channel and both be read.

## What the envelope chose

Three of the "fixed proportions" are not design decisions at all. They are the
smallest values that keep the audit clean at the extremes, and each was found by
running the audit rather than by looking at a picture:

- **`BRANCH_R0 = 0.85`** — the innermost bead has to clear the trunk (radius
  0.36 plus a full-size bead 0.46) *and* its neighbours around the fan. Five
  branches at 0.85 leave a 1.00 chord between them, which a 0.46 bead clears.
- **`TRUNK_BASE = 0.80`** — a bare lower stem that carries no data whatsoever.
  It exists because 3.33 units of branch reach at −24° falls 1.35, and the
  lowest hub has to be higher than that plus a bead radius or the worst record's
  branches go through the floor. **The palm grew a stem because the data has a
  worst case.** That is what a design envelope does to a design.
- **`BEAD_STEP = 0.62`** — beads on one branch are *meant* to touch; that is the
  necklace pattern. So the audit's rule is "no part's centre inside another's
  silhouette", not "no overlap at all".

## The audit

`build_glyph_envelope.py` runs two checks on all three probes and refuses to
write the CSV if either fails:

1. **Nothing below the baseline** the planter box stands on.
2. **No part's centre inside another part's silhouette.**

Both use `gv_extents._support()`, the same per-geometry support function the
checker uses. A bounding sphere would call a trunk segment 0.65 wide because it
is 1.30 tall, and report the whole fan of branches as buried inside it — which
is precisely the false-alarm class that hand-rolled previewers generate.

The generic checker (`code/gv_extents.py`) catches the faults *any* scene can
have. This design-specific audit catches the fault *this* design can have.
Both, on the three probes, before generating all N. The output of the first is
committed in `extent_report.txt`.

## A second envelope: Global Scale

`extent_report.txt` also holds the report at Global Scale 3, and it is not
clean — the bare stem's centre ends up inside the planter box. That is not a
bug in this file, and it cannot be authored away:

> Global Scale multiplies every glyph's rendered **size**, and the placement
> radius of children on surface-riding topologies (Sphere, Torus, Cylinder,
> Cube, Pin). It does **not** move Cartesian children, roots, or World Grid
> children, whose translates are literal coordinates.

This glyph composes entirely from Cartesian offsets — deliberately, because
Cartesian offsets cannot shear and cannot surprise you. The price is that at a
high Global Scale its parts grow into each other. A glyph built on surface-riding
placement scales coherently instead, because size and seating ride the same
multiplier. Neither is wrong; the choice has to be made knowingly, which is why
the report at both settings is committed rather than only the flattering one.

## Occlusion, honestly

In the maximum glyph the trunk segments are largely hidden behind the fronds.
That is a real cost and it is accepted here rather than hidden: the segment
colour is a *summary* of the four branches in front of it, so what the
occlusion costs is recoverable by orbiting, or by clicking a segment and reading
its label. If a channel were unique to the trunk, the design would have to move
it — an occluded part that carries a value nothing else carries is not a
trade-off, it is a defect. See `references/hyperglyph-design.md` §6.

Regenerate with `python build_glyph_envelope.py`. Never hand-edit the CSV.
