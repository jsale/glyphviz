# Examples Gallery

Every scene below ships in the GlyphViz examples set — download it once and every page's *Try it* line is one drag-and-drop away. Most examples also include the generator script that produced them, so they double as recipes for visualizing your own data.

[:material-download: Download the examples (GlyphViz_Examples.zip)](https://iluvdata.org/glyphviz/examples/GlyphViz_Examples.zip){ .md-button }

*~289 MB download, ~702 MB extracted*

You can also take just one. Every page below has its own download button, and the examples that hold several scenes offer one zip per scene — most are a few megabytes. Each single-example zip contains that scene's files at its top level: extract it and you have one self-contained folder, or drag the zip onto the GlyphViz window and it extracts beside itself and loads.

Install notes and good first scenes: [Installation → Example scenes](../getting-started/installation.md#example-scenes).

## San Diego River Watershed Atlas

One watershed, built up in phases: its terrain and river network first, then the weather, water and fire that cross it, all on the same map.

- **[The Watershed, Source to Sea](sd-river-watershed.md)** — 433 square miles of real relief, the whole river network by stream order, gauges and rain stations
- **[The Pulse: A Century of the San Diego River](sd-river-pulse.md)** — river flow and mountain rain, month by month since 1912, with the whole century on a panel beside it
- **[The Storm Winter, 2004–05](sd-river-storm-2005.md)** — one of the wettest winters on record, day by day: 18 rain gauges across the county and the river answering each storm
- **[The May 2014 Fire Siege](sd-fire-siege-2014.md)** — eight fires and a Santa Ana as a space-time cube: 128 weather stations, 2,654 fire alerts and every fire at its real place and moment
- **[The Burn Record: San Diego County Wildfires, 1910–2025](sd-fire-history.md)** — every mapped fire since 1910, laid down as layers: ground that keeps burning rises into striped stacks, coloured by era
- **[After the Cedar Fire](sd-cedar-runoff.md)** — every storm since 1960 on a burned creek and an unburned one, measured against what that storm normally gave: the first rain on the burn scar ran 46 times higher
- **[Santa Ana Wind, December 2012](santa-ana-wind.md)** — 130 weather stations every ten minutes for a month, as the wind swings offshore and the county dries out
